Hello! And welcome to an introduction to Real Analysis. In these notes, I will be following Analysis I, by Terrence Tao.

What exactly is Real Analysis? Fundamentally, it is the study of our familiar number systems in a proof-oriented, formal approach. It is also a study of how exactly these numbers interact with each other.

For example, here are some of the questions we will be able to answer after this study:

1. What is a "real" number? Is there a largest number? What is the smallest number other than $0$? Why does $2$ have a square root but not $-2$? What is the difference between natural, integer, rational, and irrational numbers?
2. What is a sequence, and what is a limit? Which sequences have limits and which don't? Can you add infinite numbers together and get another number?
3. What is a function? What is calculus, including differentiation and integration? Can you differentiate or integrate an infinite sum of functions?

Ok great! But say you already know how to answer these questions, or in other words you know the "what" of Real Analysis. What we also need is the formal "why" of Real Analysis, in order to answer the edge cases and tricky cases.

For example, let's say you take three numbers $a$, $b$, and $c$. You may be tempted to say that $a \times c = b \times c$ always implies that $a = b$. However, what if $c = 0$? then, $a \times 0 = b \times 0$ is always true for any numbers $a$ and $b$, and $a = b$ is not true for any two arbitrary numbers.

This is a simple example, so let's take a more interesting one. Say you have an infinite sum:

$$
S = 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + ...
$$

Let's ignore for a moment that we haven't quite learned how to handle infinite sums like this. We will assume we can handle it like any other equation. This means that we can multiple both sides by the same number, and add and subtract equations from each other:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2S &= 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 + ... \\
&= -1 + \big( 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + ... \big) \\
&= -1 + S \\
\Rightarrow S &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

So the sum of only positive integers results in a negative number. A bit surprising! Clearly something went wrong here, but what? Real Analysis will help us answer such questions!
